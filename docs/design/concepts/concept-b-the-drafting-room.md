# Concept B · The Drafting Room — the work-order-first chassis

One self-contained mockup: [concept-b-the-drafting-room.html](concept-b-the-drafting-room.html).
A **sim**: nothing here writes. The clamped app on the plate — *Ledger · SIM*, an invoices app in
Angular — is someone else's app being worked on, drawn from its own surveyed gauges, never from
Jig's tokens. Every fake says `sim`; the SIM strip at the top is the only clock on the page.

## The bet

**The loop is the product, and the plate is evidence.** Mark → work order → the shop → trial
fit is what a UX partner and an engineer hold together; the running app is what they point at
while holding it. So the room is arranged around the *work orders*: a left spine of cards
stacked by state along a visible ladder (marked · drafted · released · in the shop · trial fit),
a centre column that opens the chosen order's two faces, and the plate on the right showing the
clamped app *in relation to that order* — its marks lit, everything else veiled quiet. The shop
is a narrow lane between the faces and the plate, because that is where a released order goes
before it comes back as a trial fit. The logbook runs along the bottom as a ribbon, because
provenance is the signature of the whole room, not a panel you open.

The second half of the bet is that **release is the one demand**. Every other move on the
bench is light — click to mark, type on the human face, pick a card — and one move has weight:
RELEASE is held for ~800 ms under a filling ring, ember, alone on the screen. Let go early and
nothing happens, said in words. The shop face is blank until then, on purpose.

## What it champions

- **F5 · files under `.jig/`** — the state line on every released order names its file
  (`.jig/work-orders/0005-customer-picker-finds-a-customer-by.md · sim · nothing written`); a
  sketch names `.jig/sketches/…`. Jig never edits app source: the *after* frame is the shop's
  work, labelled as such.
- **F6 · the work order's two faces** — the human face (what · why · where · acceptance ·
  fixture) is editable until release; *where* is filled from the survey (component · selector ·
  file · screen), never typed. The shop face is filled on release in the app's own idiom
  (Angular 20 · standalone · signals · SCSS · Jest — as surveyed): files to touch, patterns the
  survey found, API, tests to write. Both faces stay visible together.
- **F7 · the drafter seam, pull never push** — a new mark drafts itself after a labelled cost
  (*drafting · local model · ~3s · sim*, a bar in the card and the faces). The SIM strip's
  `drafter` pin swaps Ollama for *none*: the mark then waits, says why (*no local model — the
  drafter pin is off*), and offers *fill the human face yourself* or *draft again*. #0008 is
  the seeded case where Ollama did not answer. The agent-as-drafter path is not shown.
- **F8 · the app through the proxy, the loupe injected** — the plate header says so. Hover
  outlines the component and names it (`InvoiceRowComponent · app-invoice-row ·
  invoice-row.component.ts`) by walking up to the nearest *surveyed selector*, which is what a
  click resolves to (standing in for `ng.getComponent`). Marks are stored as selector + nth
  and measured live, so they survive reflow and the trial fit's narrower frames.
- **F9 · Sketch is a card type** — #0004 *Payment reminders* is a work order whose plate is a
  sheet: a dot grid on the app's surface colour, boxes dashed in the app's ink, a hotspot to a
  second sheet, the primary action outlined in the app's primary. The loupe marks sketch boxes
  the same way; the palette makes a new blank sheet.
- **F10 · fixtures two ways without touching app code** — *invoices-12 · from InvoiceDto ·
  recipe 4021* is generated by a seeded PRNG in the page (deterministic; reload gives the same
  twelve). Applied: the list shows twelve rows each suffixed *(fixture)* and the form fills
  with labelled fake data; the frame cap says *fixture invoices-12 · recipe 4021 · sim*;
  `printed` returns the app's own data. *customers-8* is listed and says it is not wired.
- **F11 · the trial fit as a fate mirror** — a fitted order splits the plate: *before — as
  clamped* beside *after — with #0001 · trial fit · built 26m ago · sim*. The after frame
  carries a scripted change for five components (days-late labels, per-currency outstanding,
  email in the picker, relative due date, a clear affordance) and a dashed *changed here* mark
  for the rest. *toolpath · 3 clicks · replay* walks list → detail → form on both frames.
- **F12 · Gauges, two-way lit** — a tab on the plate's edge opens a drawer *in flow*: the
  frames narrow rather than being covered (Law I.7). Click *border* and every surveyed use of it
  on the current screen lights on the plate with a dashed storm hairline (measured: 2 on the
  detail screen, the shell and the nav; more on the list); hover a component on the plate and
  its gauges light in the drawer (3 for the detail component). Categories: colour · type · space · radius · shadow · motion · z, each
  with its use count and the survey's age.
- **F13 · register** — the `:root` block is `tokens/starter.css` verbatim (see Register);
  Obsidian & Ember, dark.
- **F14 · the tongue** — every shop word is paired at first encounter where it first appears:
  *Work orders — one change each: a human face and a shop face*; *Plate — the app, clicked*;
  *loupe — point at anything to see what it is; click to mark (a spot with a request
  attached)*; *the shop — connected agents*; *release = approve: the shop face is filled and
  the file is written*; *Gauges — the design system, measured and categorized*; *Fixture — a
  reproducible set of test data*; *trial fit — before, beside after*; *toolpath — a recorded
  click sequence*; *Sketch — a screen that does not exist yet*; *Scrap bin — removed, counted,
  regenerable*; *Logbook — everything that happened on the bench*; *bench (one per clamped
  repo)*; *survey (the read of the repo)*. The banned column does not appear in the UI (grep;
  the one *approve* is the pairing itself). "Connected" is the commission's own word for an
  agent (§2, §3), kept.
- **F15 · React + Vite** — the page is plain HTML/JS by the prototyper's rule, but the chassis
  is four panels and a lane, each a component with one render function over one state object
  — the shape a React bench would take.
- **F18 · nothing leaves the machine** — the SIM strip says *nothing here writes · reload =
  the bench again*; with the shop *away*, a released order waits in `.jig/work-orders/` and
  the lane says so.
- **F20 · the cut list, as a loop you can click** — survey (helm) → gauges (drawer) → plate
  with loupe → mark → drafted → release → the shop picks it up (▸, sim) → trial fit → fixture
  fills a form. Toolpath and Sketch are present as stretch.
- **F21 · non-goals held** — no code is generated on the bench; the after frame is the shop's;
  no cloud, no accounts, one user.
- **Law II across the room** — the logbook ribbon carries time · age · actor for every event;
  scrapping moves an order to the bin (counted in the ribbon's corner, listed in a popover,
  regenerated in one gesture to the state it left); cards fade with age and remain; the survey,
  every card, every log entry and the after frame state their age against the frozen clock;
  `+10m` on the SIM strip ages the room so the fading can be seen.
- **Law III** — every acknowledgement is a class or text change in the same frame; the two
  processes that run longer than 300 ms (drafting ~3 s, building ~4 s) show a bar with a stated
  cost; nothing moves at rest; `printed` exists on the spine's filters, the logbook's filters,
  the plate, and the fixture drawer. Ctrl/⌘ K opens *find*: one move to invoke, one to name
  (43 items at rest — every work order, every gauge, every fixture, every action).

## The viewport

Designed for **1440×900 CSS** and opened there. Columns: spine 296 · faces 368 · shop lane 72 ·
plate 636 (measured `grid-template-columns: 296px 368px 72px 636px`); the app frame inside the
plate is 574 px wide alone, **280 px per frame in the trial fit** and 288 px with the Gauges
drawer open — Ledger renders its compact layout at those widths through a container query
(nav collapses to glyphs, the No. and Due columns drop, days-late wraps under the chip). Rows:
SIM strip 28 · helm 48 · deck · logbook 76. RELEASE sits at y 684–724 inside the faces panel
(88–812) at rest; the faces body scrolls above it.

## Register

The `:root` block is `design-book/tokens/starter.css` **verbatim** — header comment, every
token, the reduced-motion override (diff against the file: empty, 85 lines). A second, labelled
block follows it: **the clamped app's gauges** (`.lg-scope` — Ledger's primary, accent, warn,
surfaces, ink, border, font, radius, elevation, motion), scoped to the plate and the Gauges
drawer, never used by Jig's chrome. These are the survey's values — the thing the Gauges panel
shows — and they are the only raw colours after the token block (grep: the remaining hex
strings are the drawer's *displayed* survey values and work-order numbers). Derived values,
named where derived: the veil is `--bg0` at 0.42 through an SVG mask with holes for the lit
marks; the oath ring is a 22 px circle with a 62.83 dash.

Obsidian & Ember. **Ember appears on one control** — RELEASE (its hairline, its label, its
ring, its soft tint) — and only while a drafted order is chosen. **Storm is connection**: the
loupe outline, the lit marks and their numbered tags, the shop lane's left hairline while an
agent is connected, the gauge lights. **Wyrd is the shop**: the lane's tint, the picked-up
chips, the shop-face title, the *changed here* mark. **Gold never fills**: a 1 px ring around
the number of any order whose oath was held (released · in the shop · trial fit). Warn is the
SIM tag and the in-flight spine of a *marked* card. Nothing moves at rest.

## Honest costs / frictions

- **The chassis spends width on the order and starves the plate.** 574 px for a real Angular
  app is narrow, and the trial fit halves it to 280. Ledger has a compact layout because I
  wrote one; the work app may not, and then the plate scrolls sideways or the mirror has to
  stack (top/bottom) or the frames shrink the app below the 11 px floor. This is the concept's
  first cost and it is structural: a plate-first chassis pays the opposite price.
- **The veil recolours the subject.** *Lit, everything else quiet* darkens the app to 42 %; a
  designer checking the app's real colours needs `printed` or hand mode first. The alternative
  — outlining only — loses the *in relation to this order* reading the bet depends on.
- **The loupe is a mode.** In *loupe*, clicks mark; in *hand*, clicks go to the app. A mode can
  be forgotten; the plate's status line always says which one is on, but a modifier-click would
  be less discoverable and less honest, so I kept the mode.
- **Drafted cards carry no demand colour.** The commission's one-mass law reserves ember for the
  RELEASE control, so three drafted orders waiting on a human look calm on the ladder; only
  their rung position and the *drafted* word say they are yours. Matter may want the waiting to
  read louder; the concept says the ladder is the loudness.
- **The plate is not under the one-mass law.** Ledger's blue bar and red *Overdue* chips are
  saturated masses on the screen and are the subject's own; Jig's chrome has exactly one. Stated
  because a screenshot count would find more than one.
- **The shop lane is 72 px and reads vertically.** Two agents and three held orders would crowd
  it; a real bench would need the lane to grow or become a drawer. Its advance button is a
  *sim* control standing in for MCP traffic.
- **The invented costs are wrong in life.** *~3 s* drafting and *~4 s* building are the sim's;
  a 7B model on the 4060 will take 5–20 s, and the bar's stated cost must come from the drafter,
  not a constant.
- **Provenance on cards rides `--faint`.** *drafted · local model · sim* is 3.2:1; the state is
  also the rung and the faces header repeats it in `--dim`, so nothing is lost — but the *who*
  is quiet by design and a reader who wants it at a glance will squint.
- **Age fades cards, including drafted ones.** Law II's honest aging dims a 60-minute-old
  drafted card to 0.55 while it is still waiting on a human. RELEASE stays ember; the card does
  not. A tension between Laws I and II, filed rather than resolved.
- **No keyboard path places a mark.** Cards, filters, tabs, the hold (Space) and the palette
  are keyboard-reachable; marking needs a pointer on the plate.
- **Scrapping a released order pretends the file moves.** The sim says *to the bin*; a build
  must actually move `.jig/work-orders/NNNN.md` to `.jig/scrap/` and log it.
- **The after frame is thin for unscripted components.** Five components get a real scripted
  change; the rest get a dashed *changed here* — honest, but a reviewer will read it as
  "nothing happened".
- **A very narrow window collapses the plate column to 0** (the grid's 1fr). The page guards
  the crop against dividing by zero, but the layout below ~840 px wide is not a layout.

## What to steal even if rejected

- **The ladder spine** — state as position along one visible line, counts on the rungs, a
  3 px state spine on the card. Any chassis can carry the work orders this way.
- **The in-flow drawer** — Gauges/Fixture narrow the frames instead of covering the lit mark.
  Law I.7 as one CSS decision.
- **A pinned actions bar under scrolling faces** — the one demand never below the fold.
- **The SVG-mask veil** — *lit, everything else quiet* as a single rect with holes, no
  box-shadow trick, so item 26's count stays honest.
- **Marks as surveyed selector + nth, measured live** — survive reflow, resize and the mirror's
  narrower frames; thumbnails come from a hidden render at the plate's width.
- **The frozen clock with `+10m`** — aging you can watch in a sim without a live clock.
- **Stepwise progress under reduce** — the bar's travel lives in `no-preference`; under reduce
  it jumps in steps, so the cost still reads.
- **Right-aligned tags on the right half** — a mark at the frame's edge never clips its name.
- **`printed` on the fixture** — returning to the app's own data is one affordance, not a
  cleanup.
